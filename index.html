Sex-chromosome expression and annotated donor sex in drug-perturbation cell lines

Of 49 Tahoe-100M cell lines with a recorded donor sex, 27 lacked the expected Y-gene or XIST expression pattern. Bulk RNA sequencing from other cultures agreed with the measured patterns in 47 of 49 lines. The study compiled expression records for 1,123 cell lines.

Tests of gene-expression responses to 114 LINCS2020 compounds across 41 cell lines were inconclusive. The intervals were wide, and the XIST comparison also differed in the vehicle control.

My part was the CMap and GTEx screen that came first (50 compounds, 44 tissues) and checking the GTEx sign convention. The Tahoe-100M and LINCS2020 analyses were planned, coded and run with Claude Code and rerun with OpenAI Codex, as the manuscript’s AI-use statement describes. Dr. Ben Guebila advised on the study design.

Predicting CD19 CAR-T response with DeepTCR

At CSIR-IGIB (Aug 2025 to Jan 2026, with Dr. Sherry Bhalla) I trained DeepTCR classifiers on 239,637 TCRβ sequences from 34 patients with B-cell lymphoma, 18 of whom responded to CD19 CAR-T therapy. I also helped update the framework for Python 3.10 and TensorFlow 2.12.

My trainee report gave an AUC of 0.754. That figure came from a split by sequence, so each patient’s sequences appeared in both training and test, and it does not measure prediction for new patients. In November 2026 the model was re-evaluated with whole patients held out, using AI coding agents. Across ten five-fold evaluations the median AUROC was 0.456 (range 0.298–0.579), against 0.386 for a matched composition baseline. With 34 patients, this did not show that the model predicts response.

When does a language model call a tool it doesn’t need?

For my TARA project, two small Qwen models were given the same tools in two ways: as a plain-text list and through the model’s native tool interface. On 20 paired requests, the native interface produced more calls to tools the request did not need on both models: 19/20 against 14/20 for Qwen2.5 1.5B, and 10/20 against 0/20 for Qwen3.5 4B.

An activation-steering test at a small strength (0.01) left unneeded calls at 2/8 on a held-out Qwen2.5 set, so it does not show whether steering can help. Claude Code drafted the study design and code, and AI coding assistants helped run the experiments.

Do harm categories have different refusal directions?

For the BlueDot Impact project sprint I asked whether Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct has one refusal direction or a separate one for each kind of harmful request. I estimated a direction for each of four harm categories. They were less alike than after shuffling the category labels (mean cosine similarity 0.84 against 0.98, p = 0.003).

Each direction also shifted the model’s answers on harmless prompts (KL divergence 0.13 to 1.68), so I can’t yet say they are specific to refusal. AI coding assistants helped with the code and the write-up.
